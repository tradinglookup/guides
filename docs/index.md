# Prop firm consistency rules, with a worked example

A consistency rule limits how much of your profit can come from a single day. Firms use it to tell steady trading apart from one lucky session, and it often decides whether a payout request is accepted. Check it before you trade, not after.

## How it is usually written
The rule is a percentage. Your largest single day's profit must be no more than that share of your total profit for the period. Some firms apply it during the evaluation, some only to funded payouts, and some to both.

## A worked example (example figures)
Say the limit is 40% and your total profit is 2,000. The largest day allowed is then 800. If your biggest day was 1,000, you're over the limit.

The fix is more total profit, not a smaller day: a day that has already happened can't shrink. With a 1,000 day, the total needs to reach 2,500, because 1,000 is 40% of 2,500.

## What to check in the terms
- the percentage, and whether it's measured against profit or against balance;
- which account stages it covers;
- whether breaking it fails the account or only delays the payout.
